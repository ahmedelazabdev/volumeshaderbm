What is VolumeShader_BM? The Standard in WebGL Stress Testing

VolumeShader_BM (Volume Shader Benchmark) is one of the most renowned and mathematically brutal WebGL benchmarks in the tech hardware industry. Originally adopted by international hardware enthusiasts, mobile review channels on Bilibili and YouTube (such as Golden Reviewer and Geekerwan), and GPU benchmarkers, it has become the gold standard for measuring sustained graphics computing efficiency and thermal throttling on smartphones, tablets, and PCs.

💡 Why Volumetric Raymarching is Brutal: In typical 3D game engines, graphics cards render 2D polygons (triangles) with surface textures. In contrast, VolumeShader_BM renders a continuous, three-dimensional volume of participating media using pure mathematics. For every single pixel displayed on your monitor, a ray marches through 3D space, taking 64 to 128 mathematical samples, calculating volumetric noise, light absorption, and gradient scattering on the fly.

Raymarching Physics: The Beer-Lambert Law & 3D Noise

The core of VolumeShader_BM relies on the Beer-Lambert law ($I = I_0 \cdot e^{-\sigma \cdot d}$), which describes how light decays as it travels through a dense, foggy medium. At each ray step:

  1. Position Calculation: The ray origin advances incrementally along the camera's view vector.
  2. Density Evaluation: The GPU evaluates a multi-octave 3D Perlin/Simplex procedural noise function to determine density at that spatial coordinate.
  3. Light & Color Accumulation: Diffuse illumination from a virtual directional light source is accumulated, while density is exponentially subtracted from total transmission.

Because this workload contains virtually zero rasterization bottlenecks and zero texture memory reads, it taxes the arithmetic logic units (ALUs), register files, and execution pipelines of the GPU at near 100% saturation.

How to Interpret Your Benchmark Score & Diagnose Throttling

During the 30-second benchmark run, VolumeShader_BM captures real-time frame times and calculates a normalized Benchmark Score ($Score = \text{Average FPS} \times 100$).

FPS & Score Performance Tiers (At 1.0x Native 1080p)

  • Elite Tier (Score 12,000+ / 120+ FPS): High-end desktop GPUs (NVIDIA GeForce RTX 40/30 Series, AMD Radeon RX 7000 Series, Apple M2/M3/M4 Max/Ultra chips). Unrestricted thermals and maximum raymarching bandwidth.
  • Flagship Mobile Tier (Score 6,000 – 11,000 / 60–110 FPS): Modern flagship SoCs (Apple A17/A18 Pro, Snapdragon 8 Gen 3/4, Dimensity 9300/9400). Capable of running the shader with high fluidity.
  • Mainstream Tier (Score 3,000 – 5,900 / 30–59 FPS): Mid-range laptops with integrated graphics (Intel Iris Xe, AMD Radeon 680M/780M) and upper mid-range smartphones.
  • Budget / Low-End Tier (Score Under 3,000 / < 30 FPS): Older hardware, entry-level smartphones, or battery-saving modes.

Detecting Thermal Throttling

Watch your Real-Time FPS over the course of the 30-second run. If your device starts at 75 FPS during the first 5 seconds but steadily drops to 40 FPS by second 25, your smartphone or laptop is suffering from aggressive thermal throttling. The cooling solution is unable to dissipate the heat produced by sustained ALU activity, prompting the operating system kernel to downclock the GPU frequencies to prevent hardware damage.

How to Enable Hardware Acceleration in Your Browser

If your framerate is locked below 15 FPS or VolumeShader_BM reports a software renderer (such as SwiftShader or llvmpipe), your browser's hardware acceleration is disabled:

  1. Google Chrome & Microsoft Edge: Navigate to chrome://settings/system and toggle on "Use graphics acceleration when available". Then visit chrome://flags, search for "Override software rendering list", and set it to Enabled.
  2. Mozilla Firefox: Go to Settings > General > Performance, uncheck "Use recommended performance settings", and check "Use hardware acceleration when available".
  3. Apple Safari (iOS & macOS): Ensure Low Power Mode is disabled in battery settings, as Low Power Mode caps WebGL framerates to 30 FPS.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Why is my FPS capped at 60 FPS or 120 FPS? +
Modern web browsers synchronize rendering to your monitor's physical refresh rate via V-Sync (60Hz, 90Hz, 120Hz, or 144Hz). If your GPU is powerful enough to exceed your screen's refresh rate, increase the Resolution Scale to 1.5x or 2.0x, or set Ray Steps to 128 to stress the GPU below its V-Sync cap.
Can I rotate and inspect the 3D volume? +
Yes! Click and drag your mouse across the canvas (or touch and drag on mobile screens) to manually rotate the camera around the volumetric cloud and inspect the raymarched density from any angle.
Does VolumeShader_BM test ray tracing hardware (RT Cores)? +
No. Raymarching is an algorithmic volumetric sampling technique executed entirely via standard fragment shader compute pipelines (ALUs). It does not require dedicated DXR or hardware ray-tracing acceleration cores, making it compatible with all WebGL-capable devices.
Why does my phone get warm during the benchmark? +
Because volumetric raymarching maintains constant high compute intensity without idling, the SoC draws peak operating wattage. Heat is transferred to the phone's glass or metal chassis by design to cool down the processor.
